The idea

Bobbin is a spool. The body is the core and the two big wheels are the flanges, so the wheels are the sides of the robot. The battery hangs below the axle like a pendulum, which keeps the face upright and lets Bobbin nod, lean, wobble and "look up" at you using only its two drive motors. A soft tail is the third foot.

Why it wins

  • Cheapest possible motion: 2 motors do driving, turning, nodding and leaning. No servos, no head joint.
  • Fewest parts: 6 molded tools, 7 plastic parts, 4 screws, 1 circuit board.
  • Hard to break: the big wheels are bumpers on every side; it can bump walls, chair legs and the cat.
  • A real character: a round eye screen plus body language you can't get from a box on wheels.
  • Useful, not just cute: it's a roaming camera and microphone on your Wi-Fi.

What it does

  • Home patrol: roams while you're out and sends a snapshot when it sees a person, a pet, or hears a smoke alarm, glass or barking.
  • Pet sitter: finds the dog or cat, plays chase, and shows you how they're doing.
  • Follow + greet: meets you at the door and follows you room to room.
  • Timers, reminders, clock: on its eye, said out loud, and it comes to find you.
  • Self-docking: finds its dock by the printed marker and charges itself.
  • Pet it: a touch pad in the crown makes it purr, wiggle and blink.

Assembly (about 7 minutes)

  1. Snap the cell into the drive-shell cradle and drop both motors into their rib saddles, shafts through the side holes.
  2. Press the TPU tail anchor into its slot from the inside. Glue the speaker over the grille.
  3. Slide the tested main board into the face-shell rails. Seat the eye screen, the camera and the sensor flex.
  4. Plug in the motor, cell and speaker leads, close the two shells and drive 4 M2 screws from the back.
  5. Press a wheel onto each D-shaft, roll an O-ring into each groove. Done; it flashes over the air.

Design for molding

  • Body splits at the axle plane: both shells pull straight back, no side actions.
  • 2.0 mm nominal walls, ribs at 0.8 x wall, 1.5° draft on all pulled faces.
  • One wheel tool makes both wheels: the right one is the same part turned around.
  • The lens is in-mold decorated so one clear part makes the eye and mouth windows.
  • Every 3D-printable version is the same CAD; print shells face-down, wheels flat.
